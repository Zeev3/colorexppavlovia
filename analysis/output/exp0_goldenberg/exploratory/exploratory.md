# Goldenberg et al. (2021), Exp. 1 - what lies behind each rating (EXPLORATORY)

_Not part of the main write-up. Generated by analysis/run_all.R on 2026-09-25 19:45._

N = 50 participants, 7304 trials; the anger and happiness continua pooled.

The scale is intensity, not valence: faces were morphed from neutral to angry or from neutral to happy and rated 1-50 (stored as 100 + that value), so 101 is neutral and 150 is the most intense. 125 is only the middle of the range. A rating of 140 or more means the crowd looked very emotional; 110 or less means it looked barely emotional. The two tails are therefore not two extremes and are never pooled.

**Amplification over all trials** (the paper's measure: rating - objective, positive = rated more intense than the crowd; Study 1 reports b = 2.87).

| Number of faces | Amplification (SE) | Trials |
|---|---|---|
| 1-4 faces | +2.48 (0.35) | 2283 |
| 5-8 faces | +3.10 (0.47) | 2629 |
| 9-12 faces | +2.96 (0.46) | 2392 |

**Conditioning on an extreme rating.** `Gap` is how far the rating sat beyond the crowd behind it, in that tail's own direction, averaged within participant first. On the high tail a positive gap means the rating was more intense than the crowd; on the low tail it means the rating was *less* intense than the crowd, which is understatement, not amplification.

| Number of faces | High tail: rated more intense by (SE) | Low tail: rated less intense by (SE) |
|---|---|---|
| 1-4 faces | 8.1 (0.71) | 3.9 (0.70) |
| 5-8 faces | 12.3 (0.57) | 10.4 (0.88) |
| 9-12 faces | 12.5 (0.61) | 11.2 (0.72) |

Both tails grow with set size. That two-sided pattern is the regression signature - condition on an extreme report and the stimulus behind it sits closer to the centre, at either end - and it is not what an attentional amplification account predicts, since that account gives no reason for people to increasingly understate faint crowds as faces are added.

- low tail (rated less intense): effect of number of faces p < .001; comparing only equal rating values p < .001.
  - (5-8 faces) - (1-4 faces): +6.25, p < .001.
  - (9-12 faces) - (1-4 faces): +6.17, p < .001.
  - (9-12 faces) - (5-8 faces): -0.08, p = .995.
- high tail (rated more intense): effect of number of faces p < .001; comparing only equal rating values p < .001.
  - (5-8 faces) - (1-4 faces): +3.59, p < .001.
  - (9-12 faces) - (1-4 faces): +4.01, p < .001.
  - (9-12 faces) - (5-8 faces): +0.42, p = .756.

Caution: averages of more faces are less dispersed and ratings get noisier with set size, and both widen this gap even with no bias (in an ad hoc noise-only simulation, not part of this pipeline, noise alone reproduced most of the growth). The pattern alone does not show that people inflate more.
